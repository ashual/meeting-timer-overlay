A PRACTICAL MEETING TEMPLATE

A team update
with room for everyone.

One use case for Meeting Timer Overlay: a proposed 30-minute team-update agenda with five presenters and time reserved for decisions. The extension also supports sections defined by topic in workshops, lessons, panels, and other meetings.

Use the live meeting for changes, blockers, and decisions. Put background detail and routine status in the shared deck beforehand. This is a starting plan to adapt, rather than a measured claim about how much time your team will save.

The 30-minute agenda

TimeSectionPurpose
0:00–2:00OpeningConfirm the decisions needed and the speaker order.
2:00–24:00Five presenter updatesStart with 4:24 each; recalculate at each handoff.
24:00–29:00Discussion and decisionsResolve shared blockers; send deeper topics to a follow-up.
29:00–30:00CloseRead back each action, owner, and next checkpoint.

Import the PPTX into Drive and save it as native Google Slides. Follow the template instructions; copying a deck does not transfer the extension’s browser-local settings.

What each presenter puts in the deck

  1. What changed? One result or finding since the last meeting, with the evidence that matters.
  2. What needs attention? One blocker, risk, or decision, and who can help.
  3. What happens next? The next checkpoint and its owner.

Give each presenter a clear section title. Keep detailed graphs or technical context in the following slides, so you can show them when a question warrants it.

Protect the discussion buffer

For the agenda above, set the extension’s start time to the first presenter’s scheduled start and its duration to 22 minutes. Mark the first slide of each of the five presenter sections. Keep the opening and closing discussion outside those five timed sections.

For example, in a meeting booked from 10:00 to 10:30, set the timed presenter block to start at 10:02 for 22 minutes. It ends at 10:24, leaving six minutes for discussion and close. Setting the budget to the full 30 minutes would give those minutes to the presenter sections too.

The timer does not enforce the deadline. The facilitator still needs to move the meeting into discussion at the end of the presenter block. If the opening runs late, the fixed end time leaves a smaller presenter budget.

When someone finishes early or runs over

The first allocation is 22 ÷ 5 = 4:24. If the first presenter takes two minutes, the next gets five minutes. If the first takes seven minutes, the next gets 3:45. An early overrun reduces the time for people waiting.

Agree on a wrap-up cue before the meeting. At a handoff, move to the next marked section. Put a deep discussion into a follow-up with an owner so it does not consume the rest of the update round.

A two-meeting pilot

Use the template in one real meeting. Ask the organizer how long setup took, whether handoffs were easy, and whether the last presenter had enough time. Try it again the following week before deciding whether to keep it. An install alone does not show that the workflow fits.

Which timer fits this meeting?

A standalone countdown can work when one person facilitates every handoff. A fixed per-slide timer suits a planned pace for each slide. Meeting Timer Overlay supports sections for speakers, topics, or both in Google Slides. This guide uses a team update as one example of making the remaining budget visible.

It runs in desktop Chrome and has no native Zoom or Meet integration. If you share the meeting, check that the shared slideshow surface includes the overlay. The separate speaker-notes window is excluded.
