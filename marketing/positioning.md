# Product positioning

Updated 26 October 2026 following the maker's clarification. Use this as the reference for
website copy, listing drafts, launch posts, outreach, and future growth experiments.

## Core purpose

**Keep time. Keep the agenda moving.**

Meeting Timer for Google Slides is a free, open-source Google Slides timer for meetings with multiple
speakers or topics and a fixed end time. It helps everyone see the remaining time and
understand when to move to the next presentation, conversation, or agenda item.

An agenda section can be a speaker, a topic, a discussion, or a combination. One person can
lead several sections. Several people can speak within one topic. The time is allocated
across the marked sections, whatever those sections represent.

## The problem and intended benefit

One topic or speaker can consume a meeting's limited time and leave later items rushed or
uncovered. A visible countdown and upcoming-section queue make the tradeoff easier to see.
The intended benefit is shared time awareness and easier transitions at a fixed deadline.

The timer does not enforce a limit or interrupt anyone. It recalculates the remaining budget
at section transitions; an early overrun still leaves less time for later items. Avoid
promising that every topic will be covered or that every person receives equal total airtime.

## Current workflow

The extension runs on Google Slides in desktop Chrome. Mark the first slide of each speaker
or topic section and set the meeting's start time and duration. Reaching a marked slide
switches sections automatically. The manual next-section control supports discussion-led
agendas while the slideshow is visible. It does not listen to conversations or detect who
is speaking, and it does not run as a standalone timer over arbitrary applications.

Keep those workflow requirements visible even though the meeting use cases are broad.

## Examples of fit

- Workshops: move between introduction, exercises, discussion, and wrap-up.
- Board and committee meetings: keep agenda items within the time left.
- Panels and events: make speaker or discussion-section transitions visible.
- Lessons and training: pace instruction, activities, questions, and recap.
- Community meetings: move through updates, discussion, decisions, and next steps.
- Team updates, project reviews, and research meetings: one part of the broader audience.

These are use-case hypotheses, not validated adoption, customer stories, or promised outcomes.
A shared Google Slides deck can provide the section boundaries for any of these formats.

## Copy reference

One sentence:

> A Google Slides timer for meetings with multiple speakers or topics and a fixed end time.

Benefit:

> Make the time visible so everyone knows when to wrap up and move on.

Mechanism:

> Mark speaker or topic sections. The timer follows your slides and recalculates a share of
> the time left at each transition.

Suggested future package summary (120 characters):

> Google Slides meeting timer for speakers and topics. Make time visible, share the time left, and keep the agenda moving.

Check the actual character count before a release. The installed extension's manifest summary
and public Chrome Web Store dashboard are separate publication steps; editing marketing files
alone does not change them.

## Growth implications

Keep the initial Google Slides publishers and extension-directory leads as a discovery batch.
Their audiences do not define the product's entire market. Further outreach research should
include facilitators, workshop hosts, trainers, panel moderators, event organizers, community
organizers, and board/committee administrators, in addition to workplace and education users.
Verify named publishers and their submission routes before adding them to an actionable queue.

The weekly-update deck and team-meeting guide remain useful examples. Label them as examples,
and test additional topic-led workflows instead of making all acquisition depend on weekly
team updates. Ask pilot organizers whether timing was visible and whether transitions were
easier, along with which later agenda items were rushed or missed.
